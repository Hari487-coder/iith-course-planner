What you are actually choosing

Why slots decide everything

Every course sits in one , a letter that fixes when it meets. Two courses in the same letter clash, and no instructor will move one for you. Your core courses fill slots first; the elective has to fit in what is left.

    Reading a course code

    Two letters and four numbers. Once you can read one, you can tell at a glance whether a course is even allowed to be your engineering elective.

    EM5090
    The one that matters

    Your engineering elective's first digit must be 5 or 6. A Level 4 course looks similar and will not count. This planner only lists eligible courses, but check the digit yourself if you add one by hand.

    How to actually register in AIMS

    Walked through on a live account, 25 July 2026. The gotchas are the parts nobody tells you.

    1. Log in at aims.iith.ac.in
      Roll number and password, plus a CAPTCHA. Then Menu → Academic → Course Registration.
    2. Pick the right term
      Three terms show as "Open" at once — JUL26-NOV26, May-July (Summer) 2026, and JUL25-NOV25. You want JUL26-NOV26. Click Go For Registration on that row.
      Gotcha

      Old terms staying "Open" is the easiest way to register into the wrong semester. Check the term label before you click.

    3. Add one row per course
      Click the + beside Regular. Each click adds one row — Semester 1 means six rows: three core, two department electives, one engineering elective.
    4. Set the Elective Type first, then the course
      The course search will not open until an Elective Type is chosen — it just says "Please select elective." Core EM courses go under Departmental Core Theory, your 1-credit electives under Departmental Elective, and the engineering elective normally under Free Elective.
      Gotcha

      The dropdown offers 14 types with no explanation of which applies to you, and the wrong one can get your whole submission rejected. Ask your FA before submitting, not after.

    5. Search by course code, not by browsing
      Click the blue magnifier. The list opens at 10 per page across 60 pages. Change Search By to Course Code, type the code, hit Search.
    6. Check every course actually appears
      Only 596 of the 786 courses running this term appear in the picker. If a course returns "No records found" it is not registerable for you, even when it is timetabled and its registration window is open. That is the department's to fix, and it will not fix itself. Email them the same day.
    7. Add a comment, then Register
      Comments cap at 150 characters. After submitting, your Registration Status changes from "–" and your FA has to approve it. Check back — a rejected submission appears under Recent Submission Rejected List at the bottom, which is easy to miss.

    Two dates that disagree

    Every course record in AIMS says student registration ends 31 July 2026, 23:59. The academic calendar and the department's email say the last date to add is 3 August 2026. Work to the earlier one.

    Where AIMS trips students up

    Seven friction points, each hit firsthand while registering one student. None are the student's fault.

    Fault 01

    It does not know your curriculum

    AIMS shows every student the same 596-course list. Nothing says EM5090 is compulsory for you, or that your engineering elective must be Level 5 or 6. Those rules live in an email.

    Here: requirements are built in and counted as you tick.
    Fault 02

    You plan blind, the clash warning comes late

    The picker never shows a slot next to a course, so you choose without seeing your week. AIMS does tell you when courses clash — but by then your plan is already built around the clashing pair. The slot data sits three clicks deep, one course at a time.

    Here: the week fills as you tick, so a clash never makes it into the plan.
    Fault 03

    Courses vanish with no reason given

    190 of the 786 running courses are missing from the picker. No message, no greyed-out row — just "No records found," which reads like you typed the code wrong.

    Here: known-blocked courses are labelled, with what to do about it.
    Fault 04

    Fourteen elective types, no guidance

    Departmental Core Theory, Free Elective, FCC, Basic Engineering Skills and ten more. Nothing maps them to your programme, and the same course list appears under every one.

    Here: the type to pick is written next to each course group.
    Fault 05

    Ten results per page, sixty pages

    596 courses paginated ten at a time, alphabetically by title, so "Advanced Cell Biology" comes before anything you need. The page-size control is below the fold.

    Here: one search box over the whole catalogue.
    Fault 06

    Timetables live somewhere else entirely

    Slots are published as 25 separate Google Sheets and PDFs on the academics site, in different formats. Three are not publicly readable at all.

    Here: 135 engineering courses with slots, in one list.
    Fault 07

    Nothing you can carry

    Once registered there is no timetable view, no calendar export, no printable week. Students rebuild it by hand in a notes app, which is where copying errors creep in.

    Here: print it, copy it as text, or bookmark the link.

    Putting your timetable in your phone

    The button in section 3 downloads a calendar file with every class you picked, repeating weekly for the whole semester, plus the deadlines. It works with Google, Apple and Outlook, and asks for no account access at all.

    1. Press “Add to Google Calendar”
      A file called iith-semester-1.ics lands in your downloads. It contains one repeating event per class meeting, with a reminder 15 minutes before each.
    2. Open Google Calendar on a computer
      Settings, then Import & export, then Import. Choose the file, pick which calendar to add it to, and press Import. Doing this on a laptop is easier than on a phone.
    3. It syncs to your phone by itself
      Anything in your Google account appears on your phone within a minute. Nothing else to install.
    Worth knowing

    Import it into a new, separate calendar named something like “IITH Sem 1”. If your timetable changes you can delete that whole calendar and re-import, instead of hunting for individual events.

    Just one course? Open it with the ⓘ button and use the Google Calendar link in the drawer. That adds a single weekly meeting — the download is the one that covers everything.

    The fractal system, in plain English

    This is the part everyone finds confusing. It is actually one idea: IITH chops the semester into six pieces, and a course can occupy any run of them.

    Your semester is six blocks, not one

    Each block is about two and a half to three weeks. They are called segments. A course does not have to run the whole term — it just has to start at one segment and end at another.

    Length is the credit

    One segment is half a credit. So the number of segments a course spans is its size — there is nothing else to learn. This is what a course code like “segment 1–6” or “segment 3–4” is telling you.

    Segment 1–2 1 credit
    Segment 1–4 2 credits
    Segment 1–6 3 credits
    Segment 3–4 1 credit

    Why this affects you three times

    Your electives are 1 credit each. That is why you pick two small courses rather than one big one — each runs for two segments, roughly five weeks, not the whole term.

    Your drop deadline depends on it. A 1-credit course must be dropped within a week of its segment starting. A 3-credit course gets two weeks. Miss it and you are in the course, fine or no fine.

    A short course can become a long one later. A 1-credit overview of a subject can be followed by a deeper 2 or 3-credit version in a later semester. That is the whole point of the name: the same subject at different scales.

    What it does not mean

    It has nothing to do with difficulty, and nothing to do with which courses you are allowed to take. It is only about length. A 1-credit course is shorter, not easier.

    The slot grid

    Seven one-hour slots (A–G) and four ninety-minute slots (P–S). Every course sits in exactly one. Two courses in the same letter always clash — that is the whole rule.

    09:0010:0011:0012:00 12:5514:3016:00

    No instructor will move a slot for one student. The department says this outright, so treat the grid as fixed. Wednesday afternoon is Challenge Lectures, which is why slot F is the odd one — two mornings plus a Wednesday afternoon, where every other letter has three mornings.

    Things worth knowing

    No dropping after the add/drop period, even with a fine. Every add and drop needs your Faculty Advisor's approval, so leave time. A semester is six segments of about 2.5 to 3 weeks; one segment is half a credit, which is why a 3-credit course spans segments 1–6 and a 1-credit course spans two.

    Type conversion is one-time and irreversible, capped at 3 credits. It changes an elective from one category to another and you submit a form declaring the change. The department is explicit that it cannot be repeated. Contact office.acad@iith.ac.in.

    Grades run A+, A, A-, B, B-, C, C-, D at 10, 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5 and 4 points, with F at zero. A+ and A both carry 10. The Senate mandates no minimum attendance, but an instructor may put up to 10% of the marks on it.
